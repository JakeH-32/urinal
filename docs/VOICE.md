# URINAL voice guide

Every line of player-facing text follows this guide: venues, guys, verdicts, taglines, How To Play and UI.

## The voice: deadpan witness
Report the bathroom the way a building inspector would. The joke is in the specific detail, never in commentary about it. The narrator doesn't wink, cheer or console.

- **One joke per line.** The setup is plain; one detail carries the sting. Two jokes in a verdict cancel out.
- **Never explain the joke.** Cut any clause that restates the rule, the distance or how the player should feel. "Splash radius is two urinals. You stood inside it." stops there.
- **Never call it awkward.** The player already knows. Show the consequence: the nod, the Thanksgiving invite, the five-year-plan question.
- **Specific beats generic.** "Ken from across the street. Has measured your fence." beats "a nosy neighbor". Every guy gets a name or a role, plus one fact that makes standing near him worse.
- **Consequences are social.** Small talk, status, memory, family, office politics. The worst outcome in this game is being remembered.
- **Greens are dry approval** ("No notes."), not cheerleading. **Yellows are a raised eyebrow** ("You settled."). **Reds state the consequence flatly** ("{name} has asked about your five-year plan.").

## Writing for {name}
Red verdicts insert a full name phrase: "Gary from Accounts Payable", "Your father-in-law", "The CEO". Put `{name}` at the start of a sentence or after a colon so the capital always reads right. Names never end in punctuation. Blurbs never repeat the name.

## Hard lines (never)
- **Nothing sexual, ever.** No genitals, no anatomy, no size jokes, no innuendo, no double meanings. Nobody looks, glances, peeks or compares. Men are seen from behind, fully clothed, facing the wall.
- **The rule is about courtesy, not sexuality.** The joke is never "standing close = gay", and no line implies it ("a little familiar", "cozy", "getting close" are out). Closeness costs you small talk and reputation, nothing else.
- **No kids in the bathroom, ever,** in text or as characters. The low urinal is a fixture. Don't describe it by who it's for.
- **No slurs. Never punch down at identity** (race, religion, ethnicity, sexuality, gender identity, disability, age). Mock behavior and choices: the speakerphone, the open bar, the folder in HR.
- **No real living people.** Archetypes are fine ("The CEO", "Your pastor"). Avoid brand names unless they're the venue's whole point.
- **Gross-out stops at the splasher premise.** Splashes land on shoes and khakis, stated once. No descriptions of fluids, smells or what happens in stalls.

## Escalation
Venues climb in social stakes, not in grossness. Difficulty 1 is strangers (rest stop, DMV). 3 is people who know you (reunion, client dinner). 6 is a day you can't redo (your wedding, your uncle's funeral, your keynote). The bathroom never changes; the witnesses do.

## Banned words and props
**Carried over:** bonk/bonked, vibe(s), "energy" (as slang), main character, NPC, unhinged, feral, chaotic (as an adjective), plot twist, it's giving, the audacity, living rent free, touch grass, slay, no cap, doggo, do the math, just saying, technically, legally, "would like a word", "is judging you", "for content", "Probably." as a closing line, mop, "floor texture".

**New for URINAL:** awkward, cringe, yikes, oof, bro / bro code / dude, man cave, "personal bubble", "no homo", wingman, sword fight, "crossing streams", "eyes forward", "nature calls", "when you gotta go", "the struggle", "every man's nightmare", "Choose wisely."

**Props:** stock internet grievances (fish microwavers, reply-all, crypto bros, HOA, Ohio); tiny hats, rubber ducks, googly eyes; sentient objects with funny names.

## UI copy
Dry. At most one joke per screen, and it lives in a content slot (tagline, verdict, result title), never in buttons, toasts, labels or confirm dialogs. Buttons say what they do. How To Play and the legend are the one place clarity beats jokes: state the rule exactly, at most one joke per section.

## Examples
**Keep:**
- "Three urinals was the minimum. {name} counted."
- "The divider didn't help. {name} leaned around it."
- "The stall. Correct, and nobody will ever know."
- "Your father-in-law. Still calls you 'the boyfriend.' You've been married six years."
- "Funeral home, your uncle's"

**Never:**
- "Bro, that was so awkward." (names the feeling, slang)
- "He's going to think you're into him." (homophobic framing)
- "Eyes forward, buddy!" (looking, cheerleading)
- "Nice aim. Not." (fluid pun, explained sarcasm)

## Puns
The user asked for puns, good ones, and loves pee and poop jokes ("who doesn't love a good pee joke, or poop joke"). Keep Code Brown, Leaky Faucet and the "urine" puns. Rules:
- **Only good puns.** The pun has to work on first read with no hyphen gymnastics. Keep: "Royal Flush", "Whiz Kid", "Relief Pitcher", "Urine trouble.", "Bladder up.", "Wee the people.", "Stall tactics.", "Pee-k-a-boo."
- **At most one pun per line**, never explained, never stacked, and no more than one or two per pool. Puns go best in rank titles, taglines and verdicts.
- **Nothing anatomical.** No "main vein", "shake it off", "zip it", size or aim-at-anything jokes.
